Building Global Corn and Rice Trade Networks

From bilateral import and export reports to annual network edge lists.

How can we turn country-to-country agricultural trade records into a network? I used FAOSTAT's Detailed Trade Matrix to construct yearly corn and rice trade networks covering 1986–2024. The goal is to compare how the two commodities connect countries, not just how much they trade.

1. Choose the data

I kept two items—Maize (corn) and Rice, paddy (rice milled equivalent)—and only the Import quantity and Export quantity elements. I selected the reporter, partner, year, item, element, and value fields. Quantities are in tonnes (t).

2. Reconcile import and export records

The same flow can be recorded by either side: an importing country's import report or the exporting country's export report. I reversed the reporter and partner for export observations so both records used importer = Reporter and exporter = Partner.

I then merged records by Item, Reporter, Partner, and Year. When an import quantity was available, I used it; when it was missing, I filled it with the export report. A reported zero is not treated as missing. Identical duplicate rows were removed before merging, and the merge required one record per key.

3. Build the directed edge list

I renamed the columns to O (exporter), D (importer), Year, Item, and quantity. Rows with missing fields, non-positive quantities, or the same exporter and importer were excluded.

4. Create annual networks

Using Python's NetworkX, I created a separate directed graph for each year and commodity. Each country is a node; an edge runs from the exporter to the importer; and quantity is the edge's weight. I then calculated network density, degree-based measures, betweenness, and eigenvector centrality.

Import volume and the number of import partners are different measures: in_degree(country) counts incoming connections, whereas in_degree(country, weight='quantity') sums their tonnage. This distinction matters in the results.
